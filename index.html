    How the score works

    The score counts mutual reverts: two editors who each undid the other's edits on the same page in the same year. Each such pair is weighted by the smaller of the two editors' edit counts on the page, so a pair of heavy editors counts for more than a one-off clash. The single heaviest pair is dropped, so that two people feuding alone don't top the list. The remaining total is multiplied by the number of editors involved in mutual reverts.

    This is the “M” measure from Sumi, Yasseri, Rung, Kornai and Kertész (2011), which Yasseri and colleagues used in 2013 to rank Wikipedia's most controversial articles. Bots are excluded.

    What it measures: how much back-and-forth reverting a page saw. What it doesn't: who was right, whether the page is biased, or how many people read it.

    • Most pages never see an edit war: depending on the year, only 0.2% to 1.5% of edited pages score above zero. The site lists all page-years with a score above zero. A page missing from a year had no edit war that year.
    • Pages with equal scores share a rank. Low scores are common, so many pages tie further down the list.
    • Pages are followed by their ID, so renames don't split a page's history. Titles shown are the current ones.
    • “Kind” is the page's Wikidata type today, and is missing for some pages.
